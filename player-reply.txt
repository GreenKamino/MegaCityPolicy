Hey, thanks for the detailed report — this was a genuinely useful one, and it led to a real update. I dug into all three of the problems you described, and here is what was actually happening under the hood:

PUBLIC HEALTH STUCK AT 1
Your clinics were working, but they were being outgunned. Health penalties from missing med supplies, food and water shortages, low sanitation, and high disease risk can stack up to -6 per turn, while each clinic only adds +3 toward the recovery thresholds. So the city was losing the tug-of-war every single turn — and the game never showed you any of that.

TRANSIT OVERLOADED AT 240/80
Transit capacity only comes from specific things — maglev lines, skyrail, freightways, drone corridors, skybridges, and certain units — while load grows with your population and heavier buildings. Nothing you were building happened to add capacity, and again, the game didn't tell you which things would.

EMPLOYMENT FALLING EVERY TURN
Two causes stacked here: your population was growing faster than your job slots, and — the sneaky one — that transit overload was silently draining employment by 0.5 every turn. That drain existed in the engine with zero visibility. Fair to call that a bug in spirit, even if it was "working as coded."

WHAT'S IN THE GAME NOW (JUST SHIPPED)
- Three new breakdown cards on the City screen — Public Health, Transit Grid, and Employment. Each one lists every factor pushing the stat up or down with its exact per-turn value, plus suggested fixes that link straight to the right screen.
- Tapping the Public Health, Disease Risk, or Employment cells in the City Status Matrix jumps you straight to the matching card.
- A new Transit row in Utility Status shows your load vs capacity at a glance (CLEAR / TIGHT / OVERLOADED) and taps through to the full breakdown.
- The transit-overload drain is now called out explicitly in both the transit and employment cards, so it can never silently kill a city again.
- The in-game manual has been updated to cover all of this.

It works in both live and turn-based play. Your two collapsed cities were the test case for this, so thank you — reports like this one are exactly how the game gets better. Let me know if the new cards make the picture clear, or if anything still feels like a mystery.
